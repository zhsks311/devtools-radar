Notion vs Obsidian

On Notion Free, page history stops at 7 days; Business keeps 90, and an Obsidian vault under git has no plan-imposed cutoff at all. For ten people that is 10 × $20 × 12 = $2,400/year of Notion Business against 10 × $4 × 12 = $480/year of Sync, and the difference buys a permission model you either need or do not.

Verdict

Choose Notion Business if you need granular database permissions or private teamspaces: for 10 people, 10 × $20 × 12 = $2,400/year, including Notion Agent but not Custom Agent credits. Choose Obsidian for local Markdown notes if you can forgo permissions and built-in multi-user editing: the app costs $0, while Sync for 10 people costs 10 × $4 × 12 = $480/year billed annually. Plain files under git avoid a plan-imposed history cutoff.

1. Notion sells a server with a meter; Obsidian sells a folder with a pipe

Obsidian sells a folder. Plain Markdown on your disk, no server, no meter, and the app itself is free without limits. Notion sells shared state. Permissions, databases and agents live on their servers, and everything around them carries a budget: page history, uploads, API calls, agent credits.

Obsidian: notes are files on your disk, and Obsidian states the app is free without limits, with no telemetry.

  • Notion makes history a plan feature. Notion page history is 7 days on Free, 30 on Plus and 90 on Business.
  • Obsidian has no permission model, no server-side agent and no built-in multi-user editing; collaboration requires Sync shared vaults or third-party tooling.

2. Friday recovery: does the version you need still exist?

On Friday afternoon, test recovery before choosing a plan: does the version from before the mistaken edit still exist?

The pattern this punishes is the slow mistake. Choose a window that covers how long a mistake could go unnoticed.

The rest of Free's ceiling sits in the same place:

  • File uploads capped at 5 MB (Plus raises it to 5 GB).
  • 10 external guests.
  • A Free-plan workspace block limit that Notion announced for 2026-09-08.

Obsidian has no window to buy. A vault is plain files, so git log keeps every committed version for as long as you keep the repo.

3. Bases adds views, not permissions: who should not pick Obsidian

Bases is a core plugin, not a community add-on. It renders database views over frontmatter properties, and the data stays in your Markdown files.

  • Table and Cards: 1.9
  • List and Map: 1.10 (Map needs the Maps plugin)
  • Kanban: 1.14

The .base file is YAML and holds only the view definition:

filters:
  and:
    - file.inFolder("Projects")
    - file.hasTag("active")
formulas:
  ratio: '(price / age).toFixed(2)'
views:
  - type: table
    order:
      - file.name
      - formula.ratio

Obsidian's own docs: "Delete a base and your notes are untouched." What Bases still does not give you:

  • Permissions. Obsidian has no permission model.
  • Server-side computed properties. Bases does not replace them.
  • Multi-user edit. Shared vaults sync files; there is no built-in co-editing.

Do not pick Obsidian if you need granular database permissions or built-in multi-user editing.

4. Notion's agent is real, and so is its meter

Notion's built-in agent lives on Business at $20/member/month, next to AI Meeting Notes. Free and Plus get an AI trial only.

Notion pieceBilling, per Notion
Notion AgentIncluded in Business
Custom Agents"Free to try, then $10 per 1,000 monthly Notion credits"
WorkersBeta: custom code for agent tools and sync

Custom Agents are separately metered; do not infer a Business prerequisite. Obsidian has no server-side agent.

Notion API budgets:

  • 180 requests per 60-second window per connection, or 600/minute on Business/Enterprise; bursts allowed.
  • A per-workspace budget shared by every connection; its Retry-After can exceed a minute. It scales by plan.
  • Data-source queries capped near 3 requests/second per connection.

Obey Retry-After on HTTP 429 or 529 (service_overload); the JS SDK retries for you.

5. For ten people, $2,400 a year buys permissions or it buys nothing

Run the 10-person team through both price sheets:

  • For 10 people needing Business permissions and Notion Agent, 10 × $20 × 12 = $2,400/year.
  • For 10 people needing Plus's 30-day history, 10 × $10 × 12 = $1,200/year.
  • For 10 people wanting Obsidian Sync, 10 × $4 × 12 = $480/year, billed annually.
  • For 10 people choosing Sync plus optional commercial licences, (10 × $4 × 12) + (10 × $50) = $980/year.

Business includes:

  • SAML SSO, granular database permissions, private teamspaces.
  • 90-day page history and the Notion Agent.

Do not pay for Business solely to store private drafts if you do not need its agent, AI Meeting Notes, SSO, permissions or 90-day page history.

For 10 people, the app bill is $0 with git or iCloud instead of Sync and no optional licences; choosing licences adds 10 × $50 = $500/year.

6. Choose by permissions, then test recovery

Choose Notion Business when granular database permissions or private teamspaces are required; choose Obsidian for local Markdown notes only if you can forgo a permission model and built-in multi-user editing.

Check these today:

  1. Open page history on your most important Notion page. Can you recover the version from before a mistaken edit within your plan's 7-, 30- or 90-day window?
  2. Identify the Business permissions, private teamspaces, agent or recovery window you need.
  3. List every integration calling the Notion API. Check connection, workspace and data-source query budgets before upgrading.
  4. On Obsidian at work, explicitly accept or decline the optional commercial licence.

Code and configuration

Notion 
// The API budget is the real constraint: 180 req/min per connection
// (600 on Business+), plus a shared per-workspace budget.
async function queryAll(dbId, cursor) {
  const res = await fetch(`https://api.notion.com/v1/databases/${dbId}/query`, {
    method: 'POST',
    headers: { Authorization: `Bearer ${process.env.NOTION_TOKEN}`,
               'Notion-Version': '2022-06-28', 'content-type': 'application/json' },
    body: JSON.stringify({ start_cursor: cursor, page_size: 100 }),
  });
  // Here it breaks: bursts are allowed, sustained rate is not.
  if (res.status === 429 || res.status === 529) {
    const wait = Number(res.headers.get('Retry-After') || 60);
    await new Promise(r => setTimeout(r, wait * 1000));
  }
  return res.json();
}

What you gain, what you give up

Notion

You gain
  • Permissions, private teamspaces and SSO in the same place as the content
  • Databases with relations, rollups and formulas computed server-side
  • Notion Agent and AI Meeting Notes included from Business
  • Multiplayer editing with no sync service to run yourself
You give up
  • Page history is a plan feature: 7 days Free, 30 Plus, 90 Business
  • The API is metered: 180 requests/minute per connection, 600 only on Business and up
  • Free caps uploads at 5 MB and guests at 10
  • Custom Agents bill per 1,000 Notion credits on top of the seat price

Obsidian

You gain
  • Free without limits, no sign-up, no telemetry, data on your own disk
  • Bases is a core plugin: table, cards, list, kanban and map views over frontmatter
  • History is git history — no plan-imposed cutoff on how far back you can go
  • Sync costs $4/user/month with AES-256 end-to-end encryption, and is optional
You give up
  • No permission model and no built-in multi-user editing
  • No server-side agent, no computed properties on a server, no API
  • Collaboration arrives only through Sync shared vaults or third-party tooling
  • You own the backups, the indexing and the failure modes of your own machine

Migration path

What switching actually takes

Notion to Obsidian is a conversion, not a move: export to Markdown and CSV, then rebuild what the export cannot carry — relations become links or frontmatter, rollups become formulas, and permissions disappear entirely. Obsidian to Notion is worse in the other direction: plain files become blocks, the API becomes a 180-request-per-minute budget, and your git history stops being your history. Decide by which of those two losses you can actually live with.

Common questions

Is Obsidian Sync end-to-end encrypted?
Yes. Obsidian lists AES-256 end-to-end encryption for Sync, and Sync comes with a 7-day no-questions refund.
Can a school or nonprofit pay less for Obsidian?
Obsidian gives a 40% education/nonprofit discount on Sync and Publish. Sync and Publish also have a 7-day no-questions refund.
Do I have to hand-write retry logic for Notion's 429s?
Not in JavaScript: Notion's JS SDK retries for you and obeys Retry-After. Overloads can also come back as HTTP 529 with service_overload.
Which Notion plan lists zero data retention with LLM providers?
Zero data retention with LLM providers is listed as an Enterprise feature, alongside SCIM and audit log. Lower plans do not list it. That retention policy does not establish whether workspace content is sent to them.
