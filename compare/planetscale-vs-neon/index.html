PlanetScale vs Neon

Suspended compute costs $0 on Neon; PlanetScale's smallest HA Postgres cluster has a $15 monthly compute charge even when idle. Keep 0.25 CU awake on Neon Launch: 0.25 CU × 730 h = 182.5 CU-hours × $0.106 = $19.35/month before storage, versus $15/month for PlanetScale PS-5 HA.

Verdict

Pick Neon if your databases sleep: suspended compute costs $0, and a branch per pull request costs $1.50 a month instead of a cluster. Pick PlanetScale if production must stay awake on a fixed cluster charge, or you already run Vitess-shaped MySQL — and test Neki outside production only, because its preview excludes it.

1. PlanetScale rents you a machine; Neon bills the hours it is awake

For Postgres with idle periods or many branches, compare billed CU-hours with dedicated clusters; existing Vitess-based MySQL still has an engine constraint.

PlanetScale: capacity is a cluster you rent. Neon: compute is CU-hours, meaning compute size × hours running on Lakebase Postgres.

  • PlanetScale charges for idle cluster capacity; storage, backups and egress can add charges. Each branch runs on its own separately billed dedicated cluster.
  • Neon's paid plans charge for CU-hours used, while suspended compute costs $0. Its pricing describes Lakebase Postgres as “from Databricks”.

PlanetScale's docs say it plainly: there is no free plan.

Figures below are AWS us-east-1 list prices checked 10 November 2026, or arithmetic on them; nothing is benchmarked.

Not identical machines. A CU is about 4 GB RAM; PS-80 is listed at 1 vCPU and 8 GiB plus two replicas.

  • Scale's compute crossover: $148 ÷ (1 CU × $0.222/CU-hour) ≈ 666.67 h; choose PS-80 only if its capacity suits the workload.
  • Neon's own typical-cost rows answer "how many hours?": intermittent load (140 CU-hours + 1 GB) is $15 on Launch, high load (3,000 CU-hours + 100 GB) is $353 on Launch and $701 on Scale.
  • PlanetScale's SKU ladder answers "which size?", from PS-5 at $15 to PS-2560 at $4,529 HA on arm64.

Do not pick Neon if you must retain Vitess-based MySQL; Launch also lacks an uptime SLA. Compare billed CU-hours with the cluster charge before attributing savings to suspension.

4. Who should NOT pick PlanetScale: projects with a $0 budget

On PlanetScale every branch runs on its own dedicated cluster, billed separately and prorated to the millisecond. On Neon Launch a branch is a line item: 10 per project included, then $1.50 per branch-month, prorated hourly.

20 branches alive all monthFormulaComponent/month
Neon Launch10 included + 10 × $1.50$15 extra-branch fees
PlanetScale, PS-5 HA each20 × $15$300 cluster compute
  • Add running compute and data storage on Neon; add storage, backups and transfer on PlanetScale.
  • Millisecond proration helps PlanetScale with short-lived branches: one PS-5 HA branch costs $15 ÷ 730 h ≈ $0.02 per hour.
  • Use actual branch lifetimes. Launch suspension after five minutes can be disabled.

Do not pick PlanetScale for a $0 budget; every database requires a paid subscription.

5. Neki adds sharded Postgres, but its preview excludes production

Neki is PlanetScale's sharded Postgres, in platform preview since 10 September 2026. PlanetScale says preview is not for production, and that "some of those changes will be breaking".

Apps reach it through a Neki router over the Postgres wire protocol.

  • No single-node configuration: the smallest arm64 shard is PS-10 at $30, 1 primary and 2 replicas across 3 availability zones.
  • Routers start at $6 (NKR-0). Production router compute = router rate × observed routers ÷ 3; the rate covers the three-router baseline, one per AZ.
  • Four PS-20 shards plus NKR-20 routers: 4 × $50 + $170 × 3 ÷ 3 = $370/month, before storage, backups and network usage.

6. Pick from measured compute, branch and data charges

Choose Neon when measured CU-hours plus branch, storage and transfer charges beat PlanetScale's total. Choose PlanetScale for suitable always-on cluster compute or an existing Vitess-shaped MySQL estate.

Check this today:

  1. Pull last month's awake hours per Neon compute. For compute only: $15 ÷ (0.25 CU × $0.106/CU-hour) ≈ 566.04 h on Launch.
  2. Count branches alive at once and how long each lives, and price them as PlanetScale clusters before copying the habit.
  3. Check suspension configuration and billed CU-hours; add storage, backups and transfer before choosing.
  4. Do not run production workloads on Neki's platform preview; evaluate it only outside production.

What you gain, what you give up

PlanetScale

You gain
  • A fixed cluster charge: PS-5 HA is $15/month whether the database sleeps or not
  • Every branch is its own cluster, so a schema change never touches production
  • Postgres, Neki's sharded Postgres and Vitess for MySQL on one platform
  • Backups included up to 2x disk size and run automatically every 12 hours
You give up
  • No free plan anywhere: the cheapest database is $5/month single node
  • A branch is a second cluster, so 20 PS-5 HA branches bill $300/month
  • Storage past 10 GB per instance bills at a region rate the docs expose only through an endpoint
  • Neki is a platform preview, and PlanetScale says not to run production on it

Neon

You gain
  • Suspended compute costs $0, so a database that sleeps most of the day costs almost nothing
  • A permanent free plan: 100 CU-hours, 1 GB storage and 100 projects
  • Branches cost $1.50 a month after 10 included, which makes a preview per pull request affordable
  • Storage at $0.35/GB-month and 500 GB egress included per project on Launch
You give up
  • An always-on 0.25 CU costs 0.25 x 730 x $0.106 = $19.35/month, above a $15 cluster
  • One CU running all month is $77.38 on Launch and $162.06 on Scale
  • Storage, instant restore, extra branches and egress are separate lines on the same invoice
  • Scale doubles the CU rate for the same compute shape

Migration path

What switching actually takes

Neon to PlanetScale gives up brevity: branches become clusters, scale-to-zero disappears and the cheapest HA Postgres is $15/month. PlanetScale to Neon restarts the clock — CU-hours accrue while the database is awake, so the setting to watch is the suspension timer rather than a cluster size, and storage plus egress move onto the same invoice. Dump and restore with pg_dump in both directions; both are Postgres underneath.

Questions that change the decision

Does Neon Launch come with an uptime SLA?
No; Launch has no SLA. The uptime SLA, HIPAA with a BAA, SOC 2 and ISO 27001/27701 arrive with Scale, along with private networking at $0.01/GB.
What does 100 GB of storage cost?
On Neon Launch it is 100 × $0.35 = $35/month. PlanetScale includes 10 GB per instance and bills the rest at a region volume rate it publishes only through an endpoint, on the primary and both replicas of an HA cluster.
How much egress is included?
Neon Launch includes 500 GB per project, then $0.10/GB. PlanetScale includes 100 GB/month on a production branch and 10 GB on development branches and PS-5 non-HA, then a region rate it does not print; ingress is free. At 1,024 GB on Neon Launch: 1,024 − 500 = 524 GB × $0.10 = $52.40/month; PlanetScale's overage rate is region-dependent and not printed.
When do PlanetScale backups add charges?
PlanetScale runs automated backups every 12 hours and includes backup and WAL storage up to twice disk size; overage costs $0.023 per GB-month on compressed size.
