# FACT SHEET — Prisma vs Drizzle ORM (verified 2026-11-10)

Rules: use ONLY numbers and product names from this sheet. Derived arithmetic must show its formula.
Mark vendor claims as vendor claims. The cold-start and bundle measurements below are a third
party's, not the vendors' — label them that way.

## The structural fact that frames this comparison
- Prisma sells the ORM's surroundings: Prisma Postgres (a database) and Prisma Compute (a runtime).
  The ORM itself is free on every plan.
- Drizzle's ORM and Kit are free and open source, and as of March 2026 PlanetScale has hired the
  entire Drizzle core team and is Drizzle's largest backer (Drizzle's own sustainability page).
  So both "ORMs" now sit next to a database vendor. Neither ORM has a per-seat price.

## Prisma ORM (sources: https://www.prisma.io/pricing, https://www.prisma.io/blog/announcing-prisma-orm-7-0-0,
https://www.prisma.io/changelog/2026-01-21, https://www.prisma.io/blog/prisma-7-ama-clearing-up-the-why-behind-the-changes,
https://www.prisma.io/docs/guides/upgrade-prisma-orm/v7)

Architecture timeline
- 7.0.0 shipped 19 November 2025: the client is Rust-free, generated by the `prisma-client`
  generator, and queries go through a TypeScript/WebAssembly query compiler plus a JS driver adapter.
- Prisma's stated results: 90% smaller bundle output, 3x faster query execution, lower CPU/memory.
- Prisma's unit comparison: the old Rust query engine was roughly 14 MB (7 MB gzipped); the new
  query compiler is about 1.6 MB (600 KB gzipped) — "85-90% reduction". (Vendor figure.)
- 7.3.0 (changelog dated 21 January 2026): `compilerBuild = "fast" | "small"` on the generator;
  `fast` is the default; raw queries (`$queryRaw`, `$executeRaw`) now bypass the compiler and go
  straight to the driver adapter.
- 7.10.1 shipped 15 October 2026. The `output` field is now required on the generator block — the
  client is no longer generated into `node_modules` by default — and the upgrade guide drops the
  old `url = "file:./dev.db"` datasource style in favour of `prisma.config.ts`.
- Prisma's own AMA (13 February 2026) concedes "cases where throughput is similar or slightly worse"
  because Prisma 7 talks to the database from JavaScript instead of a multi-threaded Rust runtime,
  and says plan caching is coming in 7.4.0.

Third-party measurement (Alex Georgiev, published 6 November 2026; two identical SQLite projects,
Prisma 6.19.3 with the Rust engine vs 7.10.1 with the Rust-free client; label as one developer's
local benchmark, not a vendor or laboratory result)
- 2,000 sequential `findUnique` calls on a warm connection: about 0.60 ms per lookup on Prisma 6 vs
  0.19 ms on 7.10.1 — a 3.1x speed-up that matches Prisma's claim.
- `node_modules`: 254.1 MiB on Prisma 6 vs 333.3 MiB on 7.10.1 — 31% bigger, not 85% smaller.
  The Rust-free client still ships a WASM query compiler per database in two sizes; his listing
  shows `query_compiler_fast_bg.sqlite.wasm-base64.js` at 4,406,183 bytes and the `small` variant at
  2,214,935 bytes, against a 2,895,111-byte SQLite WASM fallback in the old engine.
- Cold start, process start to first query: about 69 ms on Prisma 6 vs about 236 ms on 7.10.1 —
  roughly 3.4x slower, mostly in `import` (143-157 ms vs 51-70 ms) and `$connect()` (64-91 ms vs
  8.5-9 ms).

Prisma Postgres price sheet (https://www.prisma.io/pricing)
- The billing unit: 1 query = 1 operation. Operations included and overage:
  Free 200,000 ops (spend limit fixed at $0, no card) — Starter $10: 1,000,000 ops then $8 per
  million — Pro $49: 10,000,000 ops then $2 per million — Business $129: 50,000,000 ops then
  $1 per million.
- Storage: Free 2 GB, Starter 10 GB then $2.00/GB, Pro 50 GB then $1.50/GB, Business 100 GB then
  $1.00/GB.
- Databases: 50 on Free, 1,000 on paid plans.
- Connections: pooled 10 / 100 / 500 / 1,000 (Free → Starter → Pro → Business); direct 10 / 10 / 50 /
  100. Pool mode is transaction; idle timeout 60 minutes; pooled query duration limit 10 minutes.
- Backups: none on Free, daily with 7-day retention on Starter and Pro, 30-day on Business.
- Scale to zero when idle; no per-seat charge; database data transfer unlimited and included;
  paid plans carry a hard spend limit the customer sets.
- Compliance: GDPR everywhere, HIPAA with a BAA / SOC 2 / ISO 27001 on Business.

Prisma Compute price sheet (same page) — the runtime half
- Free: 1M requests, 360 GB-hours memory, 4 active vCPU-hours, 10 GB outbound per month.
- Paid: $1 per million requests; $0.006 per GB-hour provisioned memory; $0.064 per active vCPU-hour;
  $0.025 per GB outbound bandwidth.
- "The ceremony around shipping stays free": deploys, preview branches, idle time and team size are
  not billed. An idle app scales to zero and costs nothing.

Retiring line item
- The Accelerate cache is being retired on 1 December 2026. Its line items still appear on the sheet
  (60,000 operations included, then $0.018 / $0.008 / $0.006 per 1,000 by plan; egress 1 KiB per
  query included, then $0.09 per GiB; cache purge requests 5-20 per hour).

## Drizzle (sources: https://orm.drizzle.team/docs/overview, https://orm.drizzle.team/docs/drizzle-kit-studio,
https://orm.drizzle.team/docs/sustainability, https://github.com/drizzle-team/drizzle-orm/releases)

What it is
- "A headless TypeScript ORM with a head": schema in TypeScript, a SQL-like query API plus a
  relational query API, migrations generated by `drizzle-kit`.
- Dialects: Postgres, MySQL, SQLite, SingleStore, MSSQL, CockroachDB, through the standard drivers.
- Vendor claims: "exactly 0 dependencies"; the relational query API "always outputs exactly 1 SQL
  query"; "If you know SQL, you know Drizzle."
- No code generation step and no separate schema DSL: the TypeScript file is the schema.

Pricing
- Drizzle ORM and Drizzle Kit: free and open source.
- Drizzle Studio for local development: free forever, 120,000 monthly users (vendor figure);
  runs from `drizzle.config.ts` via `drizzle-kit studio`.
- Drizzle Gateway (self-hosted Studio as a Docker image or binary): free — it was a paid service and
  was made free; 350,000 container downloads on the GitHub registry (vendor figure).
- Studio Chrome extension (PlanetScale, Cloudflare D1, Vercel Postgres): free.
- Studio Component (embeddable web component for B2B products): the paid product; price not
  published. Drizzle states this was its main source of revenue "up until PlanetScale happened".
- Drizzle Studio itself is not open source; ORM and Kit are.

Release status — the dependency risk
- 1.0.0-rc.5 was published on 21 October 2026; 1.0.0-rc.1 on 30 April 2026. As of 10 November 2026
  the newest published release is a release candidate, not a stable 1.0, and the rc.5 notes list a
  dozen-plus BREAKING changes (postgres-js parsers, `undefined` in relational `where`, `withReplicas`
  default routing, `sqlite-proxy` rework, RQBv2 query strategy).

Ownership
- March 2026: PlanetScale hired the entire Drizzle core team. Drizzle's page: "PlanetScale is our
  biggest sponsor, they hired us".

## Derived arithmetic you may use (show the formula in the text)

- Prisma Postgres crossover: Starter costs $10 + $8 per million after the first million; Pro costs
  $49 with 10M included. They meet at $10 + 8(n − 1) = $49 → n = 5.875M operations/month. Above
  that, Pro is cheaper — the cheaper-looking plan stops being cheaper below 6 million queries.
- 20M operations/month on Pro: 10M included + 10M × $2 = $20 → $49 + $20 = $69/month.
- Same 20M on Starter: $10 + 19 × $8 = $162/month. The gap is $93/month = $1,116/year, bought by
  moving one plan up, not by adding infrastructure.
- Prisma Compute for one always-on small service: 0.5 vCPU × 730 h = 365 vCPU-hours × $0.064 =
  $23.36; 1 GB × 730 h = 730 GB-hours × $0.006 = $4.38; total $27.74/month of compute before requests.
- Drizzle's own bill: $0 for the ORM, Kit, Studio and Gateway. The whole invoice is whatever
  database and runtime you point it at.

## Do not claim
- Do not say Prisma ORM costs money, or that Prisma Postgres is required to use Prisma ORM.
- Do not present Prisma's 85-90% / 3x / 90% figures as measured facts; they are the vendor's.
- Do not present the dev.to numbers as a universal benchmark — say one developer, SQLite, local
  machine, and name the versions.
- Do not claim Drizzle 1.0 is released, or that it is unmaintained. It is at release candidate 1.0.0-rc.5.
- Do not claim PlanetScale bought Drizzle; they hired the team. Drizzle remains open source.
- Do not describe Drizzle Studio as open source, or Drizzle as having a paid ORM tier.
- Do not claim Prisma has no raw-SQL escape hatch: `$queryRaw`/`$executeRaw` exist and bypass the
  compiler as of 7.3.0.
